ASTROGUARD AI / PHASE 3

Research & Methodology

Research demonstration only

AstroGuard is not clinically validated, NASA-approved medical software, a diagnostic device, or flight software. It must not guide real patient or crew-care decisions.

What this prototype does

AstroGuard combines synthetic vital-sign samples, fictional crew profiles, editable personal demo baselines, subjective demo check-ins, transparent anomaly rules, short-window trend descriptions, and an accelerated communications queue. These components demonstrate an interface and data workflow; they do not demonstrate clinical accuracy.

Synthetic assumptions

  • All crew identities and measurements are fictional and generated locally.
  • Telemetry arrives only when a visible dashboard requests a five-second simulation tick.
  • Personal baselines are manually configured reference values, not learned physiological norms.
  • Fatigue, stress, and sleep-quality values use unvalidated AstroGuard 1–5 demo scales.
  • Demo thresholds and tolerances are illustrative constants. They are not NASA medical limits.
  • Communication delays are user-selected and accelerated to seconds or minutes for demonstration.

Explainable method

An experimental flag appears when a value falls outside an illustrative demo range or when its absolute difference from an editable personal baseline is greater than the configured tolerance. Trend text compares the mean of the latest six samples with the preceding six. “Stable,” “rising,” and “falling” describe those synthetic windows only; they do not estimate health risk.

Known limitations

  • No real sensors, clinical labels, validated instruments, medical supervision, authentication, encryption-at-rest, or multi-user authorization.
  • Telemetry and baselines remain process memory and reset when Flask restarts; assessments and communication records persist in local SQLite.
  • The timeline only reconstructs telemetry flags from the current rolling 48 samples.
  • Browser offline storage is device- and browser-profile-specific and should not hold real health information.
  • A report becomes delivered only when an explicit simulated synchronization occurs after its configured delay; no message leaves the computer.
  • Correlations between subjective inputs and telemetry are not causal or clinically interpreted.

Offline boundary

After one successful visit, the service worker can reopen the cached app shell. The browser retains the last health snapshot and queues self-assessments and outgoing-report requests. Live simulation, SQLite history, current timelines, baseline saves, server-generated reports, and delivery-state updates require the local Flask server.
